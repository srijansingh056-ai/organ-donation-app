Impact & Awareness

Why Organ Donation Matters

Every day, thousands of patients battle terminal organ failure waiting for a transplant. Your voluntary pledge brings hope where hope is fading.

Immediate Life Saving

Vital organs like the heart, lungs, liver, and kidneys replace failing biological systems, giving mothers, fathers, and children a brand-new lease on life.

Restoring Sight & Mobility

Corneal transplants restore vision to individuals living in blindness, while skin, bone, and heart valve grafts heal severe burn victims and congenital defects.

Solace for Loved Ones

Families find immense comfort knowing their loved one’s final chapter was one of profound generosity that echoed through surviving patients and their families.

Bridging the Critical Gap

With someone added to the transplant waiting list every 9 minutes, pledging now ensures an ethical, transparent pool of donors ready to save lives.

Truth & Science

Myths vs. Facts

Misinformation prevents many from pledging. Let's break down the most common misconceptions with scientific medical reality.

Myth

If doctors see I’m an organ donor on my ID, they won’t work hard to save my life.

Medical Fact

The medical team treating you in emergency care is completely separate from transplant recovery teams. A doctor's statutory duty is 100% focused on preserving your life.

Myth

I'm too old or have medical conditions, so my organs cannot be used.

Medical Fact

Donors in their 70s, 80s, and 90s have saved lives! At the time of passing, physicians evaluate organ viability on a case-by-case basis. Never rule yourself out.

Myth

Organ donation disfigures the body and forbids having an open-casket funeral.

Medical Fact

Organ and tissue recovery is conducted with the utmost surgical dignity in a sterile operating theatre. Clothing completely covers incisions, permitting open-casket viewings.

Myth

My religion or faith does not permit organ and tissue donation.

Medical Fact

Nearly all major world religions—including Christianity, Islam, Hinduism, Buddhism, Judaism, and Sikhism—support donation as an act of selfless charity and love.
